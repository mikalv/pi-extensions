# Psychology Analytics

Use `/psychology` after context and customer evidence exist. The goal is not to decorate copy with generic persuasion tricks; it is to explain what helps a specific customer understand, trust, start, and continue using the product.

## Frameworks

- Jobs To Be Done: functional, emotional, and social progress.
- Awareness: unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → product-aware → most aware.
- Friction: cognitive, effort, financial, social, trust, switching, and implementation costs.
- Motivation: autonomy, control, competence, belonging, status, safety, novelty, and relief where evidence supports them.
- Influence: reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, and unity.
- Proof and risk: which claims need a demo, data, peer evidence, guarantee, trial, documentation, or transparent limitation.

## Pattern

1. Read the shared context, customer research, market research, and persona artifacts.
2. Map evidence to journey stage, job, motivation, objection, and friction; label inferences.
3. Recommend only levers supported by the audience, offer, and decision context.
4. Translate each lever into a concrete message, offer, conversion, onboarding, or creative direction.
5. Flag deceptive urgency, manufactured scarcity, hidden costs, shame, fear exploitation, misleading proof, policy conflicts, and brand conflicts.
6. Save `outputs/campaigns/<slug>-psychology.md` and verify it exists.

Prefer clarity, credible proof, reversibility, and reduced effort over pressure. Never invent customer emotions, quotes, review counts, or scarcity.
